Concept Library Vivek’s growing textbook

Land Titles & Registration

Registration — why it’s everything
You legally own immovable property only once the sale deed is registered with stamp duty paid — not merely once you’ve paid for it. Block registration and you’ve effectively frozen the property. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
Prohibited list (Section 22A)
A district-wise register of survey numbers sub-registrars must refuse to register — legitimately covering government, endowment/wakf and ceiling-surplus land, but dangerous because it works on survey numbers, which can wrongly sweep up private land sharing or mistyped into a listed number. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
De-notification NEW
The formal process of striking a property off a prohibited/restricted list so it becomes transactable again — the exact step the alleged “30% commission” racket sits on top of. — taught by: today’s 22A escalation
Patta land NEW
Land for which the owner holds a formal government-issued title deed, as opposed to disputed, assigned, or government land — the category Section 22A is meant to exclude, and the category getting wrongly trapped by it. — taught by: today’s 22A escalation
Regularisation, GOs and memos
Governments legalise occupied or exempted land over the decades through Government Orders — but a regularised parcel is only clean on paper if that GO is attached to its record. Collect the GO/memo history, not just the sale deed. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
TSLR vs GHMC numbering
Hyderabad has two parallel address systems — a 1969–70 Town Survey Land Records numbering and GHMC’s own ward/block numbers. Mismatches between the two have turned private homes into “government land” on paper. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
ULC (Urban Land Ceiling)
A repealed law that once capped urban landholding; the exemption/surrender records it left behind still matter, since several exempted parcels were wrongly re-listed for want of an entered exemption. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
Waqf land
Property permanently donated for Muslim religious purposes under a central Act — state revenue officers cannot simply delete waqf entries, so colonies near old disputed waqf land face the slowest 22A-style resolution. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
Dharani vs Bhu Bharati
Dharani was the previous government’s land-records portal; Bhu Bharati is the current replacement. Errors made migrating data between the two are the government’s own explanation for the 22A crisis. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
Khata (and a khata freeze) NEW
The municipal record that puts a property on the local tax/ownership rolls — without it you can’t cleanly get a bank loan, utility connections, or resell. Freezing khata issuance on a batch of sites (as happened with 1,100 MUDA plots) makes them unsellable overnight even though the sale deed still exists. — taught by: the MUDA cancellation’s implementation
Cancellation vs title
An allotment-based title can be undone at the root: if the original government allotment was illegal, everything built on it since — deeds, registrations, loans — can collapse. Registration records a transaction; it doesn’t purify a rotten source. — taught by: the MUDA scam
Development authority (built-in conflict)
Bodies like MUDA, BDA and HMDA both make the rules (approvals) and play the game (own and sell land) — a structural conflict of interest that land scandals tend to grow from. — taught by: the MUDA scam
Alternative-site / 50:50 compensation
Paying a landowner in developed plots instead of cash when their raw land is acquired — the honest version is transparent and formula-based (like land pooling); the abused version becomes a discretionary currency for favours. — taught by: the MUDA scam
Commission of Inquiry
A judge-led fact-finding body that can recommend but not punish — its power is legitimacy: acting on a judge’s report is politically defensible, ignoring one is costly. — taught by: the MUDA scam
JDA and benami (the coercive version)
A Joint Development Agreement — landowner contributes land, developer builds, they split the result — is your core, legal business. The dirty cousin: get land blacklisted, owner turns desperate, a “partner” appears offering 50:50 through a benami (name-lender) entity. — taught by: the Section 22A saga
Restoration vs redevelopment
Restoration repairs what exists; redevelopment replaces what failed. Good heritage projects mix both instead of bulldozing everything. — taught by: the Mir Alam Mandi revamp
Phased execution
Build a section, shift occupants in, build the next — slower and costlier per square foot, but livelihoods survive the construction, unlike a clear-everyone-out-and-build-fast approach. — taught by: the Mir Alam Mandi revamp
Reserve price vs sale price NEW
A published reserve is only the floor an auctioneer will accept — the actual winning bid in a competitive e-auction can land well above it, as ITI’s own July sale of nearby land at a higher per-acre rate suggests it might here. — taught by: ITI’s Bengaluru land e-auction
PSU land monetisation / NLMC NEW
The National Land Monetization Corporation is the central vehicle that professionally values and auctions off surplus land held by old public-sector companies inside cities that grew up around their factories/offices — a leading government-supply signal for local land markets. — taught by: ITI’s Bengaluru land e-auction
RMP zoning NEW
Bengaluru’s statutory Revised Master Plan tags every parcel with a permitted use; a “Commercial (Business)” tag means a buyer can build offices without a fresh land-use conversion — a major value driver baked into the price. — taught by: ITI’s Bengaluru land e-auction

Government Money & Institutions

Revenue deficit
Daily income minus daily bills, with no asset creation either side — a state running one is borrowing just to pay salaries and pensions, like taking a loan for groceries. — taught by: Telangana’s CAG report
Fiscal deficit
The full year’s borrowing need, including money spent building assets. Borrowing to build a road can pay for itself; borrowing for groceries can’t — the ratio between the two deficits tells you which kind of borrower a state is. — taught by: Telangana’s CAG report
Committed expenditure
Salaries, pensions and interest — the untouchable first claim on every rupee. When it eats the budget, infrastructure that moves land prices fights for scraps. — taught by: Telangana’s CAG report
Devolution / Finance Commission share NEW
Every five years a Finance Commission decides what slice of central taxes each state gets. A state that grows faster and controls population better can still see its share shrink, since formulas partly weight population — Karnataka says this cost it ₹65,000 crore over six years. — taught by: today’s Southern Zonal Council
Schedule IX / Schedule X institutions NEW
Lists of government companies and institutions the 2014 AP Reorganisation Act said must be divided between Telangana and Andhra Pradesh — 23 of them remain undivided twelve years on, a long-running bureaucratic fight over shared assets. — taught by: today’s Southern Zonal Council
Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act NEW
The central law governing who can tax and regulate mineral extraction. States want more taxing power over mining within their own territory; the Centre has historically resisted for the sake of national uniformity. — taught by: today’s Southern Zonal Council

Delimitation, Water & Infrastructure

The 1971 freeze (delimitation)
Lok Sabha seat allocation between states hasn’t changed in 50+ years even as populations diverged — a fairness device for population-controlling states, now colliding with one-person-one-vote logic. — taught by: the delimitation saga

Infrastructure moves speech → feasibility study → DPR → funding → tender → construction. Land prices react at every stage, but only construction is certain — file speculative corridor talk, don’t chase it. — taught by: Naidu’s southern corridor pitch

🎓 The Section 22A saga — day 3 of the public fight

Built from: Telangana Today (BRS legal cell, 19 Aug) · Siasat (Harish Rao’s letter and Ponguleti’s reply, 20 Aug)

📖 The story so far

Every state keeps a list of land that cannot be legally sold: government land, land assigned free to the poor (who may not resell it), ceiling-surplus land, waqf (Muslim religious endowment) land, temple land. Section 22A of the Registration Act is the legal switch that tells every Sub-Registrar Office: if a property’s survey number is on this list, refuse to register it. Think of it as a blacklist wired into the registration computer.

The list is maintained by the revenue department — humans updating decades-old records. In September 2025, with roughly 6,000 prohibited-property disputes clogging the High Court, the court ordered a fresh, clean list to be prepared and published. The revenue department circulated revised lists — but while migrating records from the old Dharani portal to the new Bhu Bharati portal, it uploaded unverified data to sub-registrars without stripping out genuine private (patta) land that happened to share a survey number with government land, or without carrying over old regularisation GOs, or without reconciling Hyderabad’s two parallel numbering systems (1969–70 Town Survey Land Records vs GHMC ward/block numbers). Entire survey numbers got flagged, trapping legitimate owners — including families in HMT Housing Colony, whose layout was HMDA-approved back in 1995 — inside them. Registrations froze across Hyderabad, Ranga Reddy, Medchal-Malkajgiri and Sangareddy.

⚡ What happened now

The ground reality (19 Aug): hundreds of affected owners converged on Telangana Bhavan, BRS’s headquarters, seeking help. One case highlighted: “Rajesh” of HMT Housing Colony bought his house on a bank loan in 2017; after losing his job in 2026 he tried to sell, and discovered at registration that his survey number had been listed — despite applying at the MRO office two months earlier with no resolution. BRS announced a free 10-lawyer legal cell led by advocate Kalyan, based at Telangana Bhavan, and senior leaders — T. Harish Rao and P. Sabitha Indra Reddy among them — promised to personally push collectors for faster resolution. Congress MLA Marri Rajasekhar Reddy separately said roughly 30,000 families in his Malkajgiri constituency alone are affected.

The politics escalate (20 Aug): Harish Rao wrote an open letter to LoP Rahul Gandhi demanding: immediate dismissal of Revenue Minister Ponguleti Srinivas Reddy; an independent judicial inquiry led by a retired High Court judge; a forensic audit of every 22A addition and removal since December 2023 (when this Congress government took office); an independent anti-corruption probe; and removal of wrongly-listed properties without requiring citizens to file fresh applications. His core allegation: a “30% commission” racket, where intermediaries demand up to 30% of market value to get a property de-notified. Ponguleti hit back hard, calling BRS a failed Opposition “spreading falsehoods to create anxiety,” pointing to flaws in the BRS government’s own Dharani portal, and landing a barb — “when Harish Rao speaks of commissions, it feels like a murderer expressing condolences” — an apparent reference to BRS MLC K. Kavitha’s own past corruption troubles. Ponguleti has separately and publicly apologised for the errors: “This is definitely a mistake by our officials. I admit a few people faced hardships and I apologise to them.”

Government-side fixes already promised in recent days: a high-level committee (ministers plus officials) clearing all pending 22A applications within 15 days, with issues resolved district-by-district by end of August; a toll-free grievance line (1800-599-4788) promising a 3-day response; and — where an owner has already booked an online registration slot — a 2-working-day verification promise from CM Revanth Reddy himself.

🔮 What happens next

Watch: (1) end of August — the government’s self-imposed district-wise rectification deadline; (2) whether the 15-day committee clock is actually met; (3) whether Congress agrees to any part of the forensic-audit demand — full transparency on every 22A addition/removal since Dec 2023 would be the single best outcome for buyers; (4) whether BRS’s legal cell generates a wave of fresh litigation in the coming weeks.

Both major parties are now visibly competing to be seen helping affected landowners — a sign the political cost of this issue is being taken seriously on all sides, which raises the odds of an actual fix rather than a slow burn.

🧠 Learn the concepts (from zero)

Registration — why it’s everything. You don’t legally own immovable property because you paid for it; you own it once the sale deed is registered at the SRO with stamp duty paid. No registration means no enforceable ownership — which is why blocking registration effectively freezes a property. It can still be occupied, but never sold, gifted, or mortgaged.
The prohibited list. A district-wise register of survey numbers SROs must reject. Legitimate entries: government land, assigned land, ceiling-surplus, waqf and endowment land. The danger: it works on survey numbers — if your private plot shares or is mistyped into a listed number, you’re blocked even though the law never intended it.
De-notification. The formal process of removing a property from the prohibited list so it becomes transactable again. This is the exact step the alleged “30% commission” racket is said to sit on top of.
Patta land. Land for which the owner holds a formal government-issued title deed — as opposed to disputed, assigned, or government land. This is precisely the category Section 22A is supposed to exclude, and precisely the category getting wrongly trapped.
Dharani vs Bhu Bharati. Dharani was the previous (BRS) government’s land-records portal; Bhu Bharati is the current (Congress) replacement. The migration between the two — not the 22A list itself — is the government’s stated root cause of the crisis. Opposition says the transition simply gave cover to a deliberate scheme.
JDA and benami (the political layer). A Joint Development Agreement — landowner contributes land, developer builds, they split the result — is perfectly legal and is your core business. The alleged coercive version: get land blacklisted, owner turns desperate, a “partner” appears offering 50:50 through benami (name-lender) entities. Knowing the clean version helps you smell the dirty one.

🎯 IAS Pointer

Registration Act, 1908 — Section 22A and its 2007 amendment context; land-records digitisation and title-verification systems (GS2/GS3 — e-governance); administrative accountability when records-migration errors cause citizen hardship (GS2 — governance, transparency); the debate between a title-guarantee system (like the proposed Conclusive Land Titling) and India’s current presumptive-registration system, which this crisis illustrates the weakness of.

⭐ Make-me-a-pro: the whole thing as one story

Picture the registration office as a wedding hall with a bouncer holding a reverse guest list — anyone ON it is refused entry. That list is Section 22A, and for decades it quietly kept government and temple land from being stolen through fake sales. Then the High Court, tired of thousands of quarrels about who’s wrongly on the list, ordered a clean rewrite. The clerks rewrote it in a hurry, forgot to carry over old pardons (regularisation GOs), and mixed up two address books (TSLR vs GHMC). Respectable families — a man in HMT Housing Colony trying to sell the house he mortgaged his job security for — reached the wedding hall and the bouncer said: you’re on the list. No sale, no loan, panic.

Two competing stories are now fighting for the truth: the minister says honest mistake, fixing it in days, with a helpline and a 15-day committee to prove it. The opposition says racket — names go ON the list for free and come OFF for 30%. Both parties are now racing to set up help desks for the same victims, which tells you the political cost is real on both sides. The test that settles the argument either way is simple: does the government actually publish the full list of who was added and removed, with dates and signatures? Sunlight, not slogans, will decide who was right.

Pro-level takeaway: 22A is now the first checkbox of due diligence in Telangana — before the EC, before the deed chain. One search against the live prohibited list costs nothing; discovering it after paying an advance costs everything.

🎓 The Regional Ring Road, delayed again — reading an infrastructure tender

Built from: Telangana Today, 20 Aug 2026

📖 The story so far

The Regional Ring Road (RRR) is a proposed roughly 340-km outer expressway around Hyderabad, well beyond the existing Outer Ring Road (ORR) — conceptually Hyderabad’s answer to Bengaluru’s proposed Satellite Town Ring Road. It’s split into a northern half (Sangareddy–Choutuppal, NH161AA, 161 km) and a southern half. NHAI first floated the construction tender for the northern stretch on 11 November 2025, under the Bharatmala Pariyojana national highways programme, using a Hybrid Annuity Model contract structure.

⚡ What happened now

NHAI has extended the bid-submission deadline for the ₹8,500-crore-plus northern package for the sixth time — this time to today, 20 August 2026. Reported causes: pending approvals from the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways’ Alignment Approval Committee, and the state government pushing for the northern and southern packages to be tendered and executed together rather than staggered.

🔮 What happens next

Watch: whether NHAI actually closes bids today or issues a seventh extension; any formal Central alignment-approval announcement; and whether a combined northern+southern execution timeline gets confirmed.

👤 Who’s who

NHAI (National Highways Authority of India) — the central implementing agency running the tender.
MoRTH — Ministry of Road Transport & Highways; its Alignment Approval Committee is reportedly the source of the delay.
Telangana R&B (Roads & Buildings) department — state agency pushing for simultaneous execution of both RRR packages.

🧠 Learn the concepts (from zero)

Bharatmala Pariyojana. The Centre’s umbrella highways programme funding projects like the RRR — knowing a road sits under this scheme tells you it has national, not just state, funding and political backing.
Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM). A contract structure where the government pays roughly 40% of construction cost upfront and the rest, plus interest, in annual instalments (annuities) over the operating period, in exchange for the private contractor taking on construction and maintenance risk. It spreads the government’s cash outflow but still requires Central budget commitment — one reason approvals move slowly.
Bid-deadline extensions as a signal. Each extension usually means an unresolved approval, financing, or design question — not that the project is cancelled, but that its true construction-start date keeps sliding right. Six extensions in nine months is a meaningfully slower pace than the original schedule implied.

🎯 IAS Pointer

⭐ Make-me-a-pro

Every ring road creates two kinds of buyers: the patient ones who wait for the alignment to be finalised and land acquisition to actually start, and the speculative ones who buy on rumour years early and then wait — and wait — through extension after extension. Six deadline extensions on the RRR North tender since November 2025 is the market telling you which kind of buyer this corridor currently rewards. The professional discipline: track the acquisition percentage and the actual construction notice-to-proceed, not the announcement. When bids finally close and a contractor is named, that’s the signal worth acting on — not today’s deadline, and not the last five before it.
